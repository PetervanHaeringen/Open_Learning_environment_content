# Final assignment — Your first program

You've learned the history. You've felt algorithms. You've built programs and fixed bugs.

Now it's your turn to make something that didn't exist before.

---

## The assignment

Build a program that solves a problem you've chosen yourself.

It's allowed to be small. It's allowed to be unfinished. But it has to be yours — not a lesson example retyped, but something where you made the choices.

---

## Requirements

**The program:**
- Solves a concrete problem or does something useful or fun
- Uses at minimum: variables, a condition, and a loop or function
- Works — even if it's not perfect

**The language:**
- Choose yourself: JavaScript, Python, or something else if you already know it
- Justify your choice (see the questions at the bottom)

**The presentation:**
- Show what the program does
- Explain how it works — for someone who has never programmed before
- Tell what was difficult and how you solved it

---

## Ideas if you're stuck

You don't have to be original — a well-executed idea beats an ambitious unfinished one.

- A calculator for something specific (e.g. how many calories in a meal)
- A quiz about a topic that interests you
- A text that analyzes a pattern (e.g. counts how often a letter appears)
- A simple text adventure game ("You're standing in front of a door. Left or right?")
- A program that creates a schedule (e.g. timetable, shopping list)
- A conversion program (e.g. Celsius to Fahrenheit, euros to another currency)

---

## How to approach it

**Week 1 — Plan**
- Choose an idea
- Write down in pseudocode what the program should do
- Determine the input, the processing, and the output

**Week 2 — Build**
- Start with the smallest working version
- Extend it step by step
- Test regularly

**Week 3 — Finish and present**
- Fix the last remaining problems
- Write a short explanation (5-10 sentences) about how it works
- Prepare your oral presentation

---

## Assessment criteria

| Criterion | What counts |
|-----------|---------|
| It works | The program runs without crashing on normal input |
| Structure | Variables, conditions, and loops are used correctly |
| Understanding | You can explain what each line does |
| Choices | You made deliberate choices and can justify them |
| Reflection | You can tell what you learned and what you'd do differently |

There are no points for complexity. A simple program you fully understand beats a complex program you copied from the internet.

---

## Remember

You're standing at the beginning of a long road. This program isn't your best work — it's your first work. That's something quite different.

Ada Lovelace wrote her first algorithm for a machine that was never built.
Turing designed his first computer on paper.
The creators of Python first wrote a language that nobody knew.

Every great beginning was once someone's first step.

This one is yours.
