# Module 5 – Exploratory Testing

Exploratory Testing is one of the most powerful and most enjoyable ways to test.

You go on a journey of discovery through the software the way a real user would:
curious, creative, and without predetermined steps.

This is where testers add real value.

---

## 1. What is Exploratory Testing?

Exploratory Testing means you test by **discovering**:

you simultaneously:
- think about what you want to test
- carry it out
- and learn while you're testing

It's the opposite of a rigid script.

> You follow your eyes, your intuition, and the signals the software gives you.

### Characteristics

- You think like a user
- You click, try, and experiment
- You follow strange glitches or unexpected situations
- You investigate without a fixed list of steps

---

## 2. Why is Exploratory Testing important?

Exploratory testing often finds bugs that never appear in any document or test case.

### Problems you often only find through exploration

- Strange behavior when clicking fast
- Mobile behavior that differs from desktop
- Illogical flows
- Confusing screen transitions
- Small layout or text issues

That's why exploratory testing is a perfect complement to scripted testing.

---

## 3. Session-Based Test Management (SBTM)

During exploratory testing, you often work in short sessions.

Such a session usually has three parts:

- **Charter** — what are you going to investigate?
- **Timer** — usually 30 to 60 minutes
- **Note-taking** — what do you see, and what stands out?

### Example Charter

> Investigate the registration page for illogical flows, error messages, and mobile use.

---

## 4. How do you do Exploratory Testing?

A few practical pointers:

- Start with a clear goal
- Think like a user
- Ask questions:
  - "What happens if…?"
- Follow unexpected situations
- Note down anything that stands out
- Test both fast and slow
- Test on mobile, tablet, and other browsers

> Exploratory testing isn't chaos.
> You work purposefully, but leave room for discovery.

---

## 5. Session Report

Use a short report during or after your session.

```text
Session Report – [component] – [date]

Charter:
  What was your goal?

Duration:
  How long did you test?

Observations:
  - What did you see?
  - What strange situations came up?

Issues found:
  - Bug 1: description + reproducible?
  - Bug 2: description + reproducible?
  - Bug 3: description + reproducible?

Notes:
  Questions, ideas, doubts
```

---

## 6. Practical assignment: 60-minute Exploratory Session

You're now going to run an exploratory testing session yourself.

### Assignment

1. Choose a component:
   - registration
   - login
   - shopping cart
   - or another component

2. Write a one-sentence charter.

3. Set a timer for 60 minutes.

4. Test exploratively and note down everything that stands out.

5. Write a session report.

6. Submit at least 3 bugs as separate bug reports.

> Ask yourself regularly:
> "Where might a user get confused?"

---

## 7. Reflection

Think back on your session:

- What surprised you?
- Which bug would you never have found with test cases alone?
- Which skills came in handy?
- What would you do differently next time?
